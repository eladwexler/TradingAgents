## Idiot Investor Summary (Jensen's Take)

**Risk per AI Cake: 69/100 (was 68).**

The AI industrial build-out is still real, still accelerating, and now running into a harder wall than before — not a demand wall, but a physical infrastructure wall. The four largest cloud companies are spending $725 billion this year to build AI factories, and they literally cannot build them fast enough because the electrical grid cannot keep up. Transformer lead times — the giant electrical components that connect data centers to the power grid — have stretched to four years in some cases. About half of all planned US data-center projects this year are being delayed or cancelled because there is not enough electricity to run them. This sounds bad for AI; it is actually a sign that demand is so strong it is outrunning the physical world's ability to supply it. It extends the build-out phase, not ends it.

What moved the risk score up by one point: the Federal Reserve. At its June meeting, nine of nineteen Fed officials penciled in a rate hike before year-end — a sharp reversal from March when zero officials expected a hike. The reason is Iran-war oil prices pushing US inflation to 4.2%, the highest in three years. Interest rates going up would pressure the valuations of expensive AI stocks, which trade at roughly 39 times next year's earnings on average for the Magnificent 7. The infrastructure reality is sound and the software companies on top are still growing 37% per year on average. The serious risk is not the business — it is the price paid to own it combined with a Fed that is now leaning toward tighter policy. Own the survivors with balance sheets and real pricing power (power/electrical, networking, TSMC, cash-rich hyperscalers). Be cautious about leveraged operators and richly priced software that hasn't yet proven it can extract economic rent.
