## Stage 2 — Research Debate

**Bull Researcher:**
This is the most straightforward AI infrastructure value play in the entire universe. PEG of 1.11 means you're paying one turn of the growth rate — practically the definition of a fair valuation for a high-growth company. The $4B contract is not speculative; the customer paid $165M upfront to reserve capacity. That's real money from a real customer who needs cooling for a real data center build starting 2027. Meanwhile, the spin-off will crystallize the Climate Solutions story and likely trigger a re-rating as pure-play comps are VRT (40x fwd PE) and FIX (54x fwd PE). MOD at 26x looks cheap. The AI cooling bottleneck is not going away — GPU wattage per rack is doubling every 2 years (A100→H100→Blackwell). MOD's earnings are about to step-change.

**Bear Researcher:**
Three risks get glossed over by the bulls. First, the D/E of 48x is not a rounding error. If rates stay elevated (10Y at 4.45% per the macro dashboard) and the company needs to refinance debt during the capacity build phase, interest expense could crimp margins. Second, the trailing PE of 131x is real — the forward EPS jump to $11.35 depends entirely on the spin-off completing successfully and the $4B deal ramping on time. Supply chain headaches are already emerging (per Zacks). Third, Gavin Baker's orbital compute thesis isn't in year 5 — it's in year 3. If even one major hyperscaler pauses terrestrial data center capex, the 2027-2029 order book gets renegotiated. That $165M upfront is a fee, not an ironclad commitment against the full $4B.

**Research Manager:** Bull case is more compelling. The supply chain and leverage risks are real but manageable. The $4B deal structural visibility far outweighs a speculative orbital-compute risk. PEG 1.11 provides margin of safety even if growth disappoints. **Buy** — initiate/add.
