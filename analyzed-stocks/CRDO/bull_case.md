# CRDO Bull Case — June 26, 2026

## The Core Thesis in One Sentence

Credo is the **only publicly traded pure-play** on the single most supply-constrained layer of the AI infrastructure stack — electrical and optical interconnect — and it now controls both legs of that market, while consensus models are still pricing in *neither*.

## I. Jensen Named the Product. This Is Not Ambiguous.

At Computex on June 2, 2026, Jensen Huang stood on stage and said, verbatim:

> "This is not the domain of optics. This is the domain of **copper**. And the core differentiator here is the **electrical SerDes technology**."

That is CRDO's product. That is CRDO's core IP. Jensen was describing the NVL72 within-rack connectivity architecture — the very design CRDO is qualified and shipping into. This is not a thematic tailwind. This is a named endorsement of the exact thing Credo sells. No other publicly traded company owns this position at this power density and lane count.

The prior analysis lesson is already in the data: fading this trend on June 6 at $206.89 cost 12.8% in four days. The market did not wait for permission. **Do not make that mistake twice.**

Evercore ISI initiated on June 22 with a $325 price target and wrote that Wall Street is **"40% too low"** on the optical opportunity. That is not a minor modeling error. That is a category miss.

DustPhotonics — silicon photonics PIC capability — closed May 28, 2026. It has **not yet appeared in a single quarterly report.** Q2 FY27 (August) is the first gate. The consensus revenue models were built before CRDO was an optical company. Evercore, which has the most current information, says the street is 40% wrong. The forward P/E of 30x is anchored to stale models.

NVIDIA is going "both copper and optical" — Jensen said this explicitly at GTC and Computex. CRDO now covers **both** with in-house IP. No other connectivity pure-play does this. MRVL is a diversified semiconductor company. Credo is a laser-focused connectivity machine that just became vertically integrated on the optical PIC layer.

## VIII. What the Bears Are Actually Arguing

Bears say: customer concentration, DCF overvaluation, insider selling.

**Customer concentration:** True. Also true of every AI infrastructure pure-play at this stage. NVIDIA had one customer (gaming) for years. The hyperscaler building AI clusters is not going to rip out its connectivity layer mid-cycle. Switching costs in SerDes qualification are enormous — 12-18 month re-qual cycles. This is a moat, not a risk.

**DCF overvaluation:** A DCF on a company with 157% revenue growth and negative terminal value assumptions is a garbage-in exercise. No DCF model built in 2024 predicted $437M quarters by 2026. Discounting CRDO's cash flows at pre-AI capex assumptions is not analysis — it is anchoring to a world that no longer exists.

**Insider selling:** Systematic 10b5-1 plans are set months in advance and are tax-driven, not conviction-driven. The CEO, CTO, CFO, and COO are all holding substantial equity positions even after selling. They are not exiting. They are diversifying. This is the most misread signal in growth investing.
