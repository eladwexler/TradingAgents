## Stage 2 — Research Debate

**Bull Researcher**: Quebec hydro power is genuinely rare and valuable — data centers need power more than they need anything else. KEEL has large, permitted, low-cost hydroelectric capacity in a stable jurisdiction. This is not replicable. If hyperscalers and AI labs are willing to sign 10-year compute leases (and they are — look at the Oracle, CoreWeave, and Microsoft data center contracts), KEEL's physical stack becomes worth multiples of today's price. The $458M raise buys time to execute. The short interest is enormous — a lease signing triggers a squeeze that could take this to $15-20 in days. Don't count them out.

**Bear Researcher**: Power without tenants is just expensive infrastructure. KEEL has been operating since Bitfarms days with zero signed AI compute leases despite months of pivot announcements. The "$458M AI data center deal" that caused the surge was a FINANCING event, not a customer deal — investors were misled by ambiguous headlines. Every day without a signed lease is $65-130M of cash out the door. At this burn rate, the next capital raise happens before leases generate revenue, and that raise will be dilutive because the stock will have repriced down from $6.29. The fundamental case is Burning (no path): -21% gross margins, -144% operating margins, -$127M FCF in the most recent quarter. How does a company priced at $3.8B justify that on fundamentals?

**Bull rebuttal**: The revenue mix was always going to be in transition — you don't build a data center for AI and expect revenue on day one. The $458M raise explicitly extended runway past the lease negotiation period. Trust the process.

**Bear rebuttal**: "Trust the process" is not an investment thesis at $3.8B. Processes that require multiple dilutive capital raises to survive to the revenue phase destroy shareholder value even when they ultimately work. The equity holders take all the downside; the debt holders get paid first.
