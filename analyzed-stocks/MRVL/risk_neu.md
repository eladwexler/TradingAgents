### 2. Is 0.7× Sizing + Entry Zone the Right Calibration?

**The math on position survivability:**

- Operational stop at $230 from preferred entry $270: **−14.8% loss**
- Operational stop at $230 from current $281: **−18.2% loss**
- ATR 9.7% = $27.35 per day; 2× ATR = $54.70 → from $270, a 2× ATR gap takes you to ~$215 — **below the operational stop, inside the thesis-break zone**

This is the critical calibration problem: **the $230 operational stop is within reach of a single bad earnings gap**. With ATR 9.7%, a Q2 miss could gap the stock −20% to −30% in pre-market, opening below $210 and triggering the thesis-break level before the market even opens. The stop at $230 is not guaranteed to be actionable.

**Beta 2.28 implications:**
- If SPY corrects 10% (normal correction), MRVL historically moves ~22–23% — that's a round-trip from $270 to ~$210, at the thesis-break level
- A Warsh-driven rate shock (e.g., 10Y to 4.75%+) historically compresses growth multiples by 15–25%; at forward P/E 45.56×, the multiple compression alone could account for a 20–35% drawdown independent of fundamentals

**Neutral verdict on 0.7× sizing:** The 0.7× governor is directionally correct but potentially insufficient given the specific combination of: (a) earnings binary in August with gap risk, (b) insider selling at the entry zone, (c) macro rate risk that beta 2.28 amplifies severely, and (d) an unnamed $1.27B acquisition that is an unquantified liability. A strictly calibrated position-sizer working from ATR and max acceptable portfolio loss would likely arrive at 0.5–0.6× absent the August earnings overhang. The 0.7× is defensible post-earnings, not before.

### 3. Single Most Important Risk Management Rule

**Pre-define your earnings gap response before August — in writing, before the session opens.**

Not the stop at $230. Not the thesis-break at $210. Those levels are moot if MRVL opens at $195 on a Q2 miss. The single rule that matters most here is:

> **Before Q2 earnings (August 2026), reduce to 0.5× or lower. If the trade is meaningful size, consider a defined-risk structure (e.g., protective put at $230) to eliminate gap risk through the binary. Do not hold 0.7× through an earnings binary on a stock with 9.7% ATR and recent insider selling at current levels.**

The Q2 report is simultaneously the thesis catalyst (must confirm $2.7B + Q3 guide $3.0B+) and the highest-probability single-event risk. Holding full-size through a binary that has a defined miss condition, on a stock that moves 9.7% in a normal day, is inconsistent with the careful sizing discipline the proposal otherwise demonstrates.

### 4. What a Genuinely Neutral Observer Says About Key Assumptions

**Assumption 1: "Jensen named MRVL the next trillion-dollar AI chip stock" is a thesis anchor**
A neutral observer notes this is a verbal endorsement at a stage event, not a supply agreement, not a disclosed contract. NVIDIA's historical pattern is to maintain multi-vendor optionality. Treating this as a durable competitive moat rather than a promotional signal is an optimistic interpretation. The thesis needs this to translate into contractual CPO wins for Feynman — that has not been confirmed.

**Assumption 2: 35% probability on bull $513 (+82% from $281)**
Getting from $281 to $513 in 24 months requires MRVL to maintain its current 45.56× forward P/E *while growing revenues 40–50% YoY*. In a rising-rate environment with potential Warsh hikes, that multiple is more likely to compress than expand. A neutral observer would reduce the bull probability or lower the bull price target to account for multiple contraction — the bull case as specified requires *both* fundamental outperformance and multiple preservation, which are partially inversely correlated in a tightening cycle.

**Assumption 3: The base case at $285 is fair**
The base case is essentially the current price in 24 months. This implicitly says MRVL is fairly valued today. If so, the only rational reason to own it is to bet on the bull scenario materializing. A neutral observer would ask: why not wait for a setup where the base case itself provides 20–30% upside and the bull case is additive? The current setup is "we need the bull case or we go nowhere."

**Assumption 4: Insider selling is an isolated signal**
CEO + CFO + President all selling at $270–300 during the same window as the proposed entry is not a one-off. These are the three people with the clearest view into Q2 execution. A neutral observer would weigh this more heavily than the proposal does — it is the most direct counterargument to "enter at $265–275." The proposal acknowledges it but files it under "bear case." In isolation it should be filed under "entry calibration."

**Assumption 5: The $1.27B acquisition is a known unknown**
The target is unnamed. A neutral observer cannot underwrite this into either scenario. It could be accretive (buys a photonics/optical IP company that accelerates the CPO thesis) or dilutive (overpays for a marginal asset to deploy the preferred stock proceeds). At $1.27B against a net debt of $1.12B, this is not a rounding error — it's a portfolio-scale bet within the company. The proposal treats it as background noise; a neutral observer would treat it as an open variable that argues for sizing conservatism until disclosed.
